# Slide Composition

## Storyline contract

Write: "By the end, **[audience]** should **[outcome]** because **[central takeaway]**."

Choose an audience-led arc such as context to stakes to evidence to action, question to analysis to answer, problem to options to recommendation, or current state to future state. An agenda is navigation, not narrative.

For every slide record its narrative purpose, message-led headline, primary message, dominant evidence or visual, evidence IDs, logical links to adjacent slides, and speaker-note need. Open with purpose, stakes, tension, or a useful question. Close with a decision, action, synthesis, or productive next question.

## Slide families

Title, opening proposition, agenda, section divider, full-bleed photograph, single statistic, statement, three-part argument, comparison, process, system diagram, timeline, map, chart-led insight, quote, case study, recommendation, roadmap, team, partners, call to action, closing, and appendix.

## Composition rules

- Allocate one narrative job and one dominant hierarchy.
- Place the message-led headline in a stable reading zone.
- Let one visual or evidence form dominate; avoid equal-weight mosaics.
- Reserve source and footer zones before laying out content.
- Use large labels and direct annotations at projection distance.
- Split dense evidence or move detail to notes or appendix.
- Change silhouette through scale, media, and grid spans while preserving the system.

## Representative sequence

Test an opening, evidence slide, explanatory slide, image or statement slide, and closing or recommendation. Read it as a sequence, not five isolated layouts.

## Anti-patterns

Reject report paragraphs, UI-card grids, repeated hero images, unlabeled diagrams, illegible sources, wrapped one-line headlines, generic "Thank you" endings, and decorative visuals with no narrative job.
