# Synthetic community connectivity evidence base

> All people, places, organisations, and figures in this fixture are synthetic and exist only to test document-production workflows.

The Riverbend Community Network pilot combined local technical training, two solar-powered access hubs, device-support sessions, and a community governance committee.

During the six-month pilot, 120 residents completed introductory network training and 18 completed the advanced maintenance pathway. The two hubs recorded 4,800 unique visits. A follow-up survey of 80 participants found that 62 reported using the connection for education, 41 for income-related activity, and 55 for public or financial services. Multiple responses were allowed.

Participants continued to report device affordability and the cost of data beyond the free hub allowance as barriers. The follow-up period was short, there was no comparison group, and self-reported uses should not be treated as verified income or education outcomes.

The recommended next phase is to strengthen local maintenance, test an affordability mechanism, and extend outcome measurement to twelve months.
